Email details — reading the results

The metrics of a sent email — delivered, opened, clicked, unsubscribed — plus the per-list breakdown and link performance

Once an email goes out, the email details screen is where you see what happened to it: how many landed, how many opened, what people clicked, and who unsubscribed. In this guide you'll read the screen and decide what to change next time.

1Open the email details

From Mailing home or from a list card, click a sent email. At the top you'll see the subject line, the send date and time, and the lists it went to. From here you can also Preview it, Duplicate it into a new email, or Send to another list.

2Read the five metrics

  • Sent — how many recipients it went out to in total, and to how many lists.
  • Delivered — how many emails actually arrived, and the delivery rate. A low rate points to bad addresses or a sender problem.
  • Opened — the share and number of recipients who opened.
  • Clicked — the share and number who clicked a link.
  • Unsubscribes — how many asked to leave the list, and what share of recipients that is.
Good to know

Always read delivery before opens. If delivery is low, the open rate is misleading — the problem is technical, not your copy. Sender details and domain verification live under Settings → Email settings, see Your account's global settings.

3Compare your lists

The breakdown by list table shows how the exact same email performed in each list separately, plus the combined total. This is the most useful comparison on the screen: same copy, same moment — so a gap between lists tells you about the audience, not the email. A list that lags consistently needs its own copy, or it's simply gone cold.

4See what people clicked

Link performance lists every link in the email and how many recipients clicked it. This is where you find out whether your main button did the work, or a side link stole the attention instead. Below it, recipient activity shows exactly who opened and who clicked what.

5Decide what to change

Three common readings:

  • Low opens — the problem is the subject line or the send time. Try something more concrete.
  • High opens, low clicks — they opened but weren't convinced. Sharpen the offer and the button.
  • High unsubscribes — frequency or fit. Make sure the audience that got it was the right one, and see Mailing lists.

What's next

Head back to Mailing home to see the trend across six weeks — a single email is a dot, the trend is the story.
